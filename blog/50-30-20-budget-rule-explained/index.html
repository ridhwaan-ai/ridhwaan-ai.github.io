What is the 50/30/20 rule?

The rule suggests spending your after-tax income like this:

  • 50% on needs: rent or mortgage, utilities, groceries, transport, insurance, minimum debt payments.
  • 30% on wants: eating out, hobbies, subscriptions, travel, gifts beyond the basics.
  • 20% on savings and debt payoff: emergency fund, goals, and extra payments on debt.

It was popularised by US senator and professor Elizabeth Warren in a 2005 book. It is a guideline, not a law, but it gives a quick sanity check on where your money goes.

Step by step: set it up in 20 minutes

  1. Find your take-home pay. Use what actually lands in your account. If your income varies, use your lowest recent month.
  2. List your fixed needs. Add rent, utilities, insurance, phone, transport and minimum debt payments. Compare the total with 50% of your income.
  3. Review the last 30 days of spending. Mark each item as a need or a want. Be honest: a bigger phone plan than you need is a want.
  4. Set the 20% first. Automate it on payday. What is left is your spending money.
  5. Check once a week. Five minutes with a simple tracker keeps you on course.

When the rule does not fit

In expensive cities, needs can take 60 to 70% of income. That is normal, not failure. Try a 60/20/20 or 70/10/20 split and keep savings as the one number you protect. If you are carrying high-interest debt, shift part of your wants into debt payoff until the balances are gone. If you earn irregularly, budget from a baseline month and treat anything above it as a bonus to save first.

Turn the 20% into goals with names

"Save 20%" is vague. A named goal with a deadline is easier to keep. Start with a small emergency buffer, then move to bigger goals such as a holiday fund, a car repair fund, or a major expense like a pilgrimage or a wedding. Common mistakes

  • Using gross pay. The rule works on after-tax income.
  • Hiding wants inside needs. Premium cable is not a need.
  • Skipping irregular costs. Car service, gifts, annual fees and school costs hit all at once. Set up sinking funds, a small monthly amount for each.
  • Giving up after one bad month. Adjust and carry on.

  • 50% needs, 30% wants, 20% savings and debt payoff, from after-tax income.

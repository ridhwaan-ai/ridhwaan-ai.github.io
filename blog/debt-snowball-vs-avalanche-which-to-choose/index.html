Debt Snowball vs Debt Avalanche: Which Payoff Method Should You Choose?

If you owe money on several accounts, the hardest part is often deciding where the next extra dollar should go. Two methods come up again and again: the debt snowball and the debt avalanche. Both work. They differ in what they optimise for, and the right one is the one you will actually stick with.

The rule they share

Whichever method you pick, you pay the minimum on every debt, every month. Then you send all of your extra money to one target debt. When that debt is gone, you roll its payment onto the next one. Missing a minimum payment costs fees and can harm your record, so never skip one to speed up another.

The debt snowball: smallest balance first

List your debts from the smallest balance to the largest. Attack the smallest first, ignoring interest rates. The first win arrives quickly, and that early win is the whole point: it builds momentum and makes the plan feel possible.

  • Best for: people who feel overwhelmed, have many small debts, or have quit plans before.
  • Trade-off: you may pay more total interest than with the avalanche.

The debt avalanche: highest interest rate first

List your debts from the highest interest rate to the lowest. Attack the most expensive one first. This costs the least over time, because you cut the fastest-growing debt before the others.

  • Best for: people who are motivated by numbers and can stay patient when the first win takes longer.
  • Trade-off: if your highest-rate debt is also large, the first payoff can take many months.

A worked example

Say you have three debts and an extra $200 a month on top of the minimums:

  • Store card: $400 balance, 24% rate
  • Car repair loan: $1,800 balance, 12% rate
  • Personal loan: $5,000 balance, 18% rate

Snowball order: store card, car repair loan, personal loan. Avalanche order: store card (24%), personal loan (18%), car repair loan (12%). Notice that the first target is the same here, so the early win comes at the same time either way. The orders only split after that. Always check your own numbers, because your debts may line up differently.

How to decide in five minutes

  1. Write down every debt with its balance, rate and minimum payment.
  2. Sort once by balance and once by rate. If the top of both lists is the same debt, start there and decide later.
  3. If the lists differ a lot, ask yourself honestly: do I need a quick win, or will I stay motivated by the savings?
  4. Pick one. Write the order down. Do not switch every month.

Make it real: find the extra money

A method is only as good as the extra payment behind it. Look at your monthly money in and out, list one-off windfalls (tax refunds, bonuses, things you can sell), and set a small amount aside as a safety fund so a surprise bill does not push you back to the cards.

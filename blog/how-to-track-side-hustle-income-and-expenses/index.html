How to Track Side Hustle Income and Expenses (A Simple Weekly System)

A side hustle can feel profitable until you add up what it costs. Materials, fees, software, fuel and advertising all quietly eat into the money coming in. The fix is not fancy software. It is a small, repeatable habit of writing things down.

This guide is general information, not tax or legal advice. Rules differ by country and region, so confirm what applies to you with a qualified local adviser.

Step 1: Keep side hustle money separate

If you can, use one bank account or card only for the side hustle. Every total becomes easier to check and every receipt easier to find. If a new account is not possible yet, mark side hustle transactions clearly so you can filter them later.

Step 2: Record every payment the day it arrives

For each payment write the date, who paid, what for, and the amount. Note any fee taken by a marketplace or payment service separately, so you can see the gross sale and the fee. Many people only record what lands in their account, and so under-count both income and costs.

Step 3: Log every business cost, and keep the receipt

Common categories: materials and stock, software and subscriptions, platform and payment fees, advertising, tools and equipment, phone and internet share, packaging and postage, business travel, and courses or training. Keep a photo of each receipt and write a receipt number next to the entry so you can find proof in seconds.

Step 4: Track mileage

If you drive for the hustle, note the date, where you went, why, and the distance. A mileage log written at the time is far more reliable than a guess made in January.

Step 5: Set tax aside as you go

Choose a percentage of each payment and move it to a separate savings pot straight away. The right percentage depends on where you live and how much you earn, so ask a local adviser. Even a rough, honest figure beats a surprise bill.

Step 6: Do a ten-minute monthly review

  1. Total your income and your expenses.
  2. Subtract to see your real profit.
  3. Note your best customer or product and your biggest cost.
  4. Decide one thing to do next month: raise a price, cut a cost, chase an unpaid invoice.

Do you need an app or a printable?

Either works if you use it. Some people like a spreadsheet; others find that writing by hand makes them more likely to keep the habit. A printable workbook has no logins and no subscription, and you can keep it on the desk next to your work.

Quick checklist

  • One account for the hustle.
  • Income logged the day it arrives.
  • Every expense logged with a receipt.
  • Mileage written at the time.
  • A fixed percentage moved to tax savings.
  • A monthly ten-minute review.

Do these six things for three months and you will know more about your side hustle than most people who have run one for years.

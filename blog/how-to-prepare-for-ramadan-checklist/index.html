How to Prepare for Ramadan 2027: A Practical 8-Week Checklist

Ramadan 2027 is expected to begin around 8 to 9 February 2027, depending on moon sighting in your area. Ramadan goes by fast, and the people who feel they got the most from it are usually the ones who prepared before the first fast. This checklist breaks the preparation into eight weeks so nothing feels rushed.

8 to 6 weeks before: set your intentions

  • Write 3 goals only. For example: finish one reading of the Quran, pray taraweeh on weekends, give charity daily. Three goals you keep beat ten you drop.
  • Be specific. "Read more Quran" is vague. "Read 4 pages after each of the 5 prayers" is a plan (20 pages a day finishes the Quran in 30 days).
  • Talk to your household. Agree who cooks on which days, when the family eats together and how late nights will work.
  • Start shifting habits. Go to bed a little earlier, cut back on caffeine, and try fasting a Monday or Thursday if you are able and it is safe for you.

6 to 4 weeks before: make your Quran plan

Divide the Quran into 30 juz and decide your daily target. If you are behind on reading, choose a smaller goal such as one juz every two days, or reading with translation. Mark the calendar with a milestone each week so you can see progress. A simple tracker with 30 boxes, one per juz, is enough.

4 to 3 weeks before: money and giving

Ramadan is often more expensive than people expect: extra groceries, hosting iftars, Eid clothes and gifts, and generous giving. Set a Ramadan budget now.

  • Estimate extra grocery spending per week and divide your usual grocery budget by it.
  • List the iftar invitations you plan to host or attend.
  • Budget for Eid gifts, clothing and travel.
  • Decide your charity plan: daily small amounts, bigger gifts in the last 10 nights, and your zakat calculation date if you pay in Ramadan.

3 to 2 weeks before: stock up and batch cook

  1. Check the pantry. Dates, rice, lentils, oats, flour, oil, spices, tea and tinned goods.
  2. Plan 4 weeks of suhoor and iftar. Repeat a small set of meals. Most families do fine with 8 to 10 dishes in rotation.
  3. Batch cook and freeze. Samosa fillings, soups, curry bases, marinated meat and dough freeze well.
  4. Write one grocery list per week and shop once, rather than daily trips that eat into your energy.

2 to 1 week before: set up the home

  • Choose a quiet corner for prayer and Quran, with the mushaf, a notebook and a water bottle.
  • Clean and declutter the kitchen so cooking is faster.
  • Prepare a visible Ramadan chart for children with simple good deeds and small rewards.
  • Adjust work and school schedules where you can, and tell colleagues what to expect.

The final week: simple tasks

  1. Write your daily routine on paper: suhoor time, prayers, work, rest, iftar, taraweeh.
  2. Prepare a dua list: family, health, forgiveness, the ummah, people you want to remember.
  3. Confirm the start date with your local moon-sighting authority.
  4. Make a plan for the last 10 nights, because that is when energy dips and the best opportunities come.

During Ramadan: stay consistent

Do a five-minute check each night: Did I pray on time? Did I read my target? Did I give something today? A weekly reflection of three lines (what went well, what to change, one dua) keeps you adjusting rather than quitting. Missing a day is not failure, so simply restart the next day.

Plan Eid early too

Eid sneaks up on people. Before the last 10 nights, decide the Eid morning schedule, the family gathering, the food, children's gifts, and who you need to visit or call. Writing it down means you can spend the last nights in worship rather than worrying about errands. After Eid, the six fasts of Shawwal are a good way to keep the momentum going.

How to Price Your Airbnb Per Night: A Simple Method for New Hosts

Set the price too high and nobody books. Set it too low and you work hard for little profit. This guide gives a simple method for choosing your first nightly rate and adjusting it with real data.

Step 1: Know your break-even price

Add up your monthly costs for the property: rent or mortgage share, utilities, internet, cleaning, supplies, insurance, platform fees and taxes. Divide by the nights you realistically expect to be booked. A new listing often starts around 40 to 55% occupancy, so use a cautious number.

Example: costs of $1,500 a month and 14 booked nights means a break-even of about $107 a night. Anything above that is profit, anything below is a loss. Try your own numbers in the free Airbnb Income Calculator.

Step 2: Study 5 to 8 comparable listings

Search your area on Airbnb using the same dates a guest would. Filter for the same number of guests, bedrooms and property type. Write down the nightly price, cleaning fee, rating and review count. Ignore listings with no reviews and luxury outliers. Look at the middle of the group, not the top.

Step 3: Price slightly below the middle at launch

New listings have no reviews, so guests need a reason to trust you. Starting about 10 to 15% under similar established listings can win your first bookings and reviews. After five or so good reviews, raise the price in small steps, around 5% at a time, and watch whether bookings slow.

Step 4: Adjust for demand

  • Weekends and holidays: raise the price on dates when local demand is high.
  • Last-minute gaps: lower the rate for nights that are within a few days and still empty. A booked night beats an empty one.
  • Long stays: offer a weekly or monthly discount if you want fewer turnovers and less cleaning.
  • Minimum stay: a two-night minimum on weekends often saves cleaning costs.

Step 5: Handle the cleaning fee honestly

Set the cleaning fee close to what cleaning actually costs you. Guests compare the total price, so a very high fee with a low nightly rate can put them off. Be clear about all fees in the listing.

Step 6: Review every month

Check occupancy and income each month. As a rough rule of thumb, if you are booked more than about 80% of available nights, you are probably underpriced. If you are under 40% after a couple of months, check your photos, title and price. Common pricing mistakes

  • Copying the highest-priced listing nearby.
  • Forgetting platform fees and taxes in your break-even.
  • Never changing the price after launch.
  • Hiding extra fees until checkout.
  • Pricing on the first day instead of testing and adjusting.

Also check the rules

Many cities require a permit or limit short-term rentals, and mortgage, lease or insurance terms may too. Confirm what applies to you before listing. This article is general information, not legal or financial advice, and figures are examples only.

Quick summary

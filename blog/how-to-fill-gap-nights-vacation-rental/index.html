How to Fill Gap Nights in a Vacation Rental (Without Slashing Your Rate)

A gap night is an empty night trapped between two bookings, too short for most guests to book. One or two a month may not sound like much. Over a year, at a $120 nightly rate, twelve unsold gap nights is $1,440 you never see. The good news: gap nights are one of the easiest losses to reduce, because you control the rules that create them.

Why gap nights happen

Most gaps come from a minimum-stay rule meeting a calendar. If your minimum is three nights and a guest checks out on Tuesday while the next guest arrives on Thursday, Tuesday night is stranded. Nobody can book one night, so it sits empty.

1. Look at the pattern before changing anything

Write down every gap night for a couple of months: the date, the day of the week, what the minimum stay was, and whether it eventually sold. You will usually see a pattern, such as gaps clustering after weekend stays. A simple log beats memory. The Vacation Rental Host Business Planner has a page for exactly this kind of record keeping, but a notebook works too.

2. Shorten the minimum stay for orphan nights

Most booking platforms let you lower the minimum stay for specific dates, or set a rule that allows shorter stays only when they fill a gap between two existing bookings. This is the highest-impact fix. A one-night booking in a gap is pure extra revenue.

3. Offer a modest gap discount

If a gap night is still empty a week out, a 10 to 15 percent discount for that night is almost always better than $0. Keep the discount small and specific to the gap. Cutting your whole calendar to fill two nights teaches guests to wait for sales.

4. Use last-minute pricing carefully

Last-minute guests are often travelling for work or have just had a plan fall through. They book fast and care less about a bargain than about convenience. Check how your nearby competitors price the same night before you drop yours.

5. Move your check-in day

If your stays keep ending on a Tuesday with nothing until Thursday, restricting check-in days (for example, Friday and Monday only in peak season) can line stays up so they stack without leaving holes. Test one change at a time so you know what worked.

6. Make the gap useful for you

Not every gap needs filling. A midweek gap is a good moment for a deep clean, a maintenance visit or restocking. Block it on purpose and use it. Our Vacation Rental Cleaning & Turnover Log includes deep-clean and maintenance pages for this reason.

7. Ask past guests

If you keep a list of repeat guests who have said they would return, a short, friendly message ("I have the 14th and 15th open at a small discount") can fill a gap without paying any platform fee. Only message people who have agreed to hear from you, and follow your platform's rules about contacting guests off-platform.

How to know if it worked

After a month, compare: how many gap nights did you have, how many sold, and at what price? Track revenue per available night rather than just occupancy, since a full calendar at a low rate can earn less than a slightly emptier one at a good rate.

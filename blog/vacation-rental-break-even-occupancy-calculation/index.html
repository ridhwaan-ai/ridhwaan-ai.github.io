Vacation Rental Break-Even Occupancy: How to Calculate It

Many hosts set a nightly rate by looking at neighbours and hoping for the best. A better starting point is knowing the lowest rate and occupancy at which your rental stops losing money. That is your break-even point, and it takes ten minutes to work out.

Step 1: Add up your monthly fixed costs

Fixed costs are what you pay whether or not a guest stays. List them for a typical month:

  • Rent or the property's running costs (utilities, internet, insurance, property tax shares)
  • Platform subscriptions and software
  • Furnishing and equipment replacement (divide yearly spend by 12)
  • Any monthly co-host or management retainer

Example: rent and utilities $1,400, internet and software $60, insurance $90, replacement budget $50. Fixed total: $1,600 a month.

Step 2: Work out your cost per booked night

Variable costs only happen when someone stays: cleaning, laundry, supplies, platform fees. Say cleaning and laundry cost $55, supplies $8 and platform fees average $12 a stay. If the average stay is 2.5 nights, variable cost per night is ($55 + $8 + $12) / 2.5 = $30.

Step 3: Find the break-even occupancy

Pick a nightly rate you think the market supports, for example $120. Each booked night then contributes $120 minus $30 = $90 towards your fixed costs.

Break-even nights = fixed costs / contribution per night = $1,600 / $90 = 17.8 nights a month.

In a 30-night month that is 17.8 / 30 = 59% occupancy. Below that you lose money; above it, every extra night is profit.

Step 4: Flip it around, the minimum rate

What if you expect only 50% occupancy (15 nights)? Required contribution per night = $1,600 / 15 = $107. Add the $30 variable cost and your minimum rate is about $137. If the local market won't pay that, you need to cut fixed costs or rethink the property.

The five numbers worth tracking every month

  1. Occupancy rate: nights booked / nights available.
  2. Average daily rate (ADR): room revenue / nights booked.
  3. Revenue per available night (RevPAN): total revenue / nights available. It is ADR times occupancy, and it shows whether a lower price that fills the calendar actually beats a higher price that doesn't.
  4. Break-even occupancy: from the steps above.
  5. Profit per night: what is left after all costs, divided by nights booked.

A quick example of why RevPAN matters

At $150 with 45% occupancy, RevPAN is $67.50. At $120 with 65% occupancy it is $78. The cheaper rate earns more, and fills gaps that otherwise sit empty. Run the numbers for your own property before changing prices, and change one thing at a time so you can see what worked.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting your own time or a co-host fee in fixed costs.
  • Using the best month's occupancy as your yearly assumption. Use a realistic average.
  • Ignoring minimum-stay rules and gap nights, which cut occupancy without showing up in rates.
  • Changing prices daily without a log, so you never learn what worked.

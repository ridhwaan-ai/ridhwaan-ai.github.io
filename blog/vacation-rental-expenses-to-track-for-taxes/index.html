Vacation Rental Expenses to Track All Year (Simple Tax-Time Checklist)

Most hosts know roughly what a booking pays. Far fewer know what the rental really earns after cleaning, supplies, fees and repairs. The difference between the two is your profit, and you can only see it if you write expenses down as they happen.

This is a practical guide to what to track and how. It is general information, not tax or legal advice: rules differ by country and region, so confirm with a qualified accountant or your tax authority.

Why a monthly habit beats a year-end scramble

Receipts fade, card statements are long, and by January you will not remember what that 43.20 charge was. Ten minutes at the end of each month is enough to log income and expenses while you still remember them. It also shows you which months are strong and which costs are creeping up.

Income to record

  • Payout amount, date and the booking it belongs to (guest name or reservation code).
  • Platform service fees taken from each payout, recorded separately from the payout itself.
  • Cleaning fees you charge guests, since they are income too.
  • Any direct bookings, deposits kept or extra charges.

Expense categories most hosts use

  • Cleaning and laundry: cleaner pay, laundry service, linen replacement.
  • Supplies: toiletries, coffee, paper goods, cleaning products, light bulbs, batteries.
  • Utilities: electricity, water, gas, internet, streaming subscriptions for guests.
  • Repairs and maintenance: plumber, handyman, appliance repair, pest control.
  • Furnishing and start-up costs: beds, sofa, kitchenware, decor, smart lock. Keep these in a separate list because they are often treated differently from everyday expenses.
  • Software and listing costs: pricing tools, channel managers, photography.
  • Insurance, licences and local taxes.
  • Travel: mileage to the property and supply runs. Keep a mileage log with date, purpose and distance.
  • Marketing and guest extras: welcome gifts, printed guest guides.

A simple monthly routine

  1. Pick one fixed day, such as the first Sunday of the month.
  2. Write every payout into your income log with its date and booking.
  3. Go through your card statement and log each rental expense in a category.
  4. Put receipts and photos in one folder or envelope labelled with the month.
  5. Add up the month: income, expenses, profit. Compare with last month.
  6. Note one thing to change: a cost to cut or a price to adjust.

Work out what each booking really earns

Take the payout, subtract the cleaning cost for that stay, supplies used and your share of utilities for the nights booked. A 120-per-night stay can leave far less than it appears if it is a one-night booking with a full clean. Seeing this per booking helps you set minimum stays and cleaning fees sensibly. Records worth keeping

  • Payout reports and monthly statements from each booking channel.
  • Receipts for every expense, with the date and what it was for.
  • A mileage log.
  • Cleaner pay records, including who was paid, when and how much.
  • Insurance, licence and local tax documents.

Check how long your local rules say you must keep records, and keep digital copies as a backup.

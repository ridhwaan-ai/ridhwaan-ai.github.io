15 Ramadan Activities for Kids at Home

Ramadan 2027 is expected to begin around 8 February, depending on moon sighting. If you have children aged roughly 5 to 10, a little structure makes the month feel special instead of long. Here are fifteen simple, screen-free ideas you can start with this week, most of which need only paper and pens.

Count the days together

  1. A moon countdown. Draw 30 small crescents on a poster or sheet of paper. Your child colors one in each evening. It gives a clear sense of progress and something to look forward to.
  2. A lantern wall. Cut paper lanterns and hang one per week, or add one for every day of fasting your child completes (even partial days count as practice).

Build kindness into the day

  1. A good-deeds chart. Pick a few small daily deeds: tidy your room, help set the table, greet a neighbor, call a grandparent. A tick each day is enough.
  2. A 30-day kindness challenge. One tiny act per day, such as sharing a snack, writing a thank-you note, or saying sorry first.
  3. A charity jar. Let children put in coins they have earned or saved, and choose together where it goes by the end of the month.
  4. Sadaqah ideas. Donate outgrown clothes and books, or bake something for the neighbors.

Learn gently

  1. Dua practice. Choose one short dua a week, such as the dua before eating, and say it together at every meal. Check wording with a teacher or parent.
  2. A Quran juz or short surah tracker. Color in each surah as it is learned or recited.
  3. The Five Pillars quiz. A short, friendly quiz at the dinner table. Make it a family game, not a test.

Involve them in iftar

  1. Let them plan one menu a week. Children choose the dessert or the salad, and help prepare it.
  2. Set the table. Give each child a job, such as dates, cups or napkins. It builds a feeling of belonging at the table.
  3. Draw the iftar plate. A good quiet activity for the last hour before sunset.

Quiet-time fillers

  1. Puzzles. Word searches, mazes and dot-to-dots are good for the tired, hungry hours in the afternoon.
  2. Coloring pages. Mosque, lantern, crescent and dates designs work well and keep small hands busy.
  3. A Ramadan journal. One line a day: "Today I felt..." or "I was kind when...".

Prepare for Eid

In the last week, make Eid cards for relatives, plan the Eid outfit and morning, and prepare small treats to hand out. Children enjoy giving as much as receiving.

A few tips for success

  • Start the week before Ramadan so the routine is ready on day one.
  • Keep it light. If a chart becomes a source of stress, simplify it.
  • Do it together. Children copy what the family does.
  • Praise effort, not perfection, especially for younger children who are practicing fasting.

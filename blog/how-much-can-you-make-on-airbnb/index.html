How Much Can You Really Make on Airbnb? A Simple Profit Formula

Most posts about hosting income quote big revenue numbers. Revenue is not profit. What you keep depends on how many nights you book, what each stay costs to turn over, and your fixed bills. This guide shows a simple way to estimate it before you commit.

Step 1: Estimate your booked nights

Occupancy is the share of nights in a month that are booked. A month has about 30.4 nights, so at 60% occupancy you book roughly 18 nights. New listings usually start lower while they collect their first reviews, so test your plan at two levels, for example 45% and 65%.

Step 2: Work out revenue per month

Revenue = booked nights × nightly rate + number of stays × cleaning fee charged. If your average stay is 3 nights, 18 booked nights is 6 stays.

Step 3: Subtract the variable costs

  • Platform fee: check your current fee on the platform. Airbnb's host-only fee has typically been around 3%, but it can change, so confirm it.
  • Cleaning cost per stay: what you pay a cleaner (or the value of your own time and supplies).
  • Consumables: toiletries, coffee, tissue, bin bags and laundry.

Step 4: Subtract the fixed monthly costs

Rent or mortgage, utilities and internet, insurance, local taxes and licences, maintenance and any software or co-host fee. Do not forget the slow items: a new kettle, a replacement towel set, a repaired lock. A good habit is to set aside 5 to 10% of revenue for maintenance.

A worked example (illustrative numbers)

Nightly rate $140, 62% occupancy, 3-night average stay, $60 cleaning fee, $55 cleaning cost, 3% platform fee. Fixed costs: $1,200 rent, $180 utilities, $60 supplies, $90 insurance and licences, $70 maintenance, $30 other.

  • Booked nights: about 18.8. Stays: about 6.3.
  • Revenue: 18.8 × $140 + 6.3 × $60 ≈ $3,010.
  • Platform fee: about $90. Cleaning cost: about $345.
  • Fixed costs: $1,630.
  • Profit: roughly $945 a month, or about $11,300 a year before income tax.

Change the occupancy to 45% and the same listing makes only about $240. That swing is why break-even occupancy matters more than the headline nightly rate.

Break-even occupancy and minimum rate

Break-even occupancy is the percentage of nights you must book to cover every cost. The minimum nightly rate is the lowest price that still gives zero loss at your expected occupancy. How to raise profit without raising prices

  1. Fill more nights: sharp photos, an honest title, quick replies and flexible minimum stays.
  2. Cut cleaning cost per night: encourage 3 to 4 night stays with a small weekly discount.

Check rules and taxes first

Many cities require a licence, limit the number of nights, or add occupancy tax. Your lease or mortgage may also forbid short-term letting. Check local rules and speak to an accountant about income tax. This article gives planning estimates only, not legal, tax or financial advice.

Quick checklist before you list

  • Know your break-even occupancy and minimum nightly rate.
  • Confirm local rules, insurance and your lease.
  • Prepare a guest guide and house rules.
  • Set a maintenance reserve of 5 to 10% of revenue.
  • Plan for a slow first two months.

Airbnb Break-Even Occupancy: How Many Nights Do You Need to Book?

Before you list a spare room or a whole home, one question matters more than your nightly rate: how many nights a month must you book just to cover your costs? That number is your break-even occupancy. This guide shows the formula, a worked example, and ways to bring the number down. Step 1: Find your profit per booked night

Not every pound or dollar of your nightly rate is yours to keep. For each stay, subtract the platform fee, the cost of cleaning the place, and supplies such as toiletries, coffee and laundry. Add the cleaning fee guests pay you.

Profit per stay = (nightly rate × nights + cleaning fee) × (1 − platform fee) − cleaning cost − supplies.
Profit per night = profit per stay ÷ nights in the stay.

Step 2: Add up your fixed monthly costs

These are the costs you pay whether or not anyone books: rent or your share of housing costs, utilities, internet, insurance, and any subscriptions for pricing or messaging tools. Leave out one-off costs for now.

Step 3: Divide

Break-even nights = fixed monthly costs ÷ profit per night.
Break-even occupancy = break-even nights ÷ days available in the month.

A worked example

Say your nightly rate is 120, the cleaning fee is 60, the average stay is 3 nights, the platform fee is 3%, cleaning costs 45 and supplies 8 per stay. A stay brings in (120 × 3 + 60) × 0.97 = 407.4, minus 53 in costs, leaving 354.4 per stay, or about 118 per night. With fixed costs of 1,200 a month, you need about 10.2 nights, or roughly 34% occupancy of a 30-day month, just to break even. Every night beyond that is profit.

How to read the result

  • Under 40%: a comfortable margin in most markets, so a slow month will not hurt.
  • 40% to 60%: workable, but check how busy comparable listings near you really are.
  • Over 60%: risky. Look at the levers below before you commit.
  • Over 100%: the numbers do not work as they stand.

These bands are rules of thumb, not data. Local demand, seasons and rules vary a lot, so compare against listings like yours in the same area.

Five ways to lower your break-even point

  1. Set a 2- or 3-night minimum. Fewer turnovers means fewer cleaning costs per night booked.
  2. Make the cleaning fee honest. It should cover the real cost of a turnover, without being so high that it scares guests off.
  3. Price smarter. Raise rates for weekends, holidays and local events. See how to price your Airbnb per night.
  4. Cut repeat questions and damage. Clear house rules and a printed guest guide save time and protect your reviews.   5. Lower fixed costs. Review utilities, internet plans and insurance each year.

What the calculator leaves out

Taxes, local permit or registration fees, furniture and the value of your own time are not included. Many cities limit short-term rentals or require a licence, so check local rules before you list.
